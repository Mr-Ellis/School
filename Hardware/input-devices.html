Input Devices
Year 9 · Hardware · How data enters a computer system

Learning Objectives

  • Understand what is meant by an input device and why it is required
  • Understand how keyboards, mice and touchscreens work
  • Understand the purpose of barcode scanners and QR code scanners
💡 Key Fact: Input devices transfer data from the outside world into a computer system for processing.

⌨️ Keyboards

A keyboard is the most common form of data entry. When a key is pressed, a small peg underneath pushes through a hole and makes contact with a conducting membrane, completing a circuit and sending a signal to the computer.

  • Advantages: Easy to use, requires little training
  • Disadvantages: Typing errors are common, data entry can be slow
  • Connection types: USB, wireless dongle, Bluetooth

🖱️ Optical Mouse

An optical mouse uses a red LED to shine light onto a surface. A sensor takes low-resolution images of the surface and compares them to detect movement and direction.

📱 Touchscreens

Resistive

Two conductive layers separated by an insulating layer. Pressure pushes them together to complete a circuit.

Stylus compatible Single touch
Capacitive

Glass layers coated in ITO that store charge. A finger changes the electric field at the touch point.

Multi-touch Finger only
Infra-red

LEDs shine IR light in a grid. Touching the screen interrupts the beams to detect position.

Durable Any input

📦 Barcode Scanners

Barcode scanners use a laser or LED to read the pattern of black and white bars. The reflected light is converted to a digital signal that identifies the product.

  • Used in retail, warehouses, libraries and ticketing
  • Can be handheld, fixed or integrated into self-checkout kiosks
  • Reads both 1D (traditional) and 2D (QR code) barcodes

📱 QR Code Scanners

QR (Quick Response) codes store data in a square grid pattern. They can hold much more information than traditional barcodes.

  • Read by smartphone cameras or dedicated QR scanners
  • Used for website links, payments, ticketing and product information
  • Can be scanned in any orientation (unlike barcodes)
