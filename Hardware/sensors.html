Lesson 3 · Sensors
Year 10 · Hardware · How computers sense the world

Learning Objectives

  • Understand what a sensor is and why it is used
  • Understand the difference between analogue and digital sensors
  • Understand the purpose of the ADC and DAC
  • Understand common types of sensors and their uses
💡 Key Fact: Sensors are input devices that convert physical quantities (temperature, light, pressure, etc.) into electrical signals that a computer can process.

  • Smart home: motion sensors (lights), temperature sensors (thermostats)
  • Car: parking sensors, tyre pressure sensors, temperature sensors

📊 Analogue vs Digital Sensors

🔹 Analogue Sensors

Produce a continuous signal (e.g., a varying voltage). Need an ADC (Analogue-to-Digital Converter) to convert the signal into digital data.

Examples: Temperature sensors, light sensors, volume controls (potentiometers)

🔸 Digital Sensors

Produce a discrete signal (e.g., a 0 or 1). They do not need an ADC because the signal is already digital.

Examples: Motion detectors (PIR), ultrasonic distance sensors, digital temperature sensors

🔄 ADC & DAC: An ADC converts an analogue signal to digital. A DAC (Digital-to-Analogue Converter) does the reverse — converting digital to analogue for output devices.

🔬 Common Sensors & Their Uses

🌡️ Temperature

Thermistor, thermocouple

Thermostats, weather stations, industrial control

Thermistor = resistance changes with temperature • Thermocouple = two different metals produce voltage when heated
💡 Light

LDR, photodiode

Automatic lighting, camera exposure, street lights

LDR = Light-Dependent Resistor • Photodiode = converts light into current
📏 Pressure

Piezoresistive, capacitive

Tyre pressure, medical devices, touchscreens

Piezoresistive = changes resistance when pressure is applied
🚶 Motion

PIR, accelerometer

Security systems, gaming controllers, smartwatches

PIR = Passive Infra-Red (detects body heat)
📡 Proximity

Ultrasonic, inductive

Parking sensors, robotics, phone screen dimming

Ultrasonic = uses sound waves to measure distance • Inductive = detects metal objects
🌧️ Humidity

Capacitive, resistive

Weather stations, agriculture, HVAC systems

HVAC = Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning

🏠 Real-World Sensor Applications

  • Smart Home: Temperature sensors for thermostats, motion sensors for lighting, light sensors for blinds.
  • Healthcare: Heart rate monitors, blood pressure sensors, pulse oximeters.
  • Manufacturing: Pressure sensors for quality control, temperature sensors for safety.
  • Transportation: Proximity sensors for parking, accelerometers for airbag deployment.
  • Agriculture: Moisture sensors for irrigation, temperature sensors for greenhouses.

💬 Class Discussion Questions:

  • Is the webcam acting as an input device or an output device?
  • What physical quantity is being detected?
  • Would the computer need an ADC if the webcam produced an analogue signal?
Click to reveal discussion answers
  • Input or output? The webcam is an input device – it captures data (video) and sends it to the computer.
  • Physical quantity: Motion (changes in the video image over time).
  • ADC needed? Yes – if the webcam produced an analogue signal, an ADC would be needed to convert it to digital data that the computer can process.

🔁 Plenary – Which Sensor?

  1. What is the difference between an analogue and a digital sensor?
  2. Why do analogue sensors need an ADC?
  3. Give three examples of sensors used in everyday life and explain what they do.
Click to reveal suggested answers
  • Analogue vs Digital: Analogue sensors produce a continuous signal (varying voltage); digital sensors produce a discrete signal (0 or 1).
  • ADC: An ADC is needed to convert the continuous analogue signal into digital data that the computer can process.
  • Examples: Temperature sensor (thermostat), light sensor (street lights), motion sensor (security systems).
