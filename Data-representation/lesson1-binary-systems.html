Binary Systems
Lesson 1 · How computers store and process numbers

Learning Objectives

  • Understand why computers use binary (Base-2)
  • Convert between denary (decimal) and binary
  • Understand bits, bytes, and the 8-bit place value grid

📖 Why Binary?

Computers use Binary (Base-2) because they are made of transistors (switches) that only have two states: ON (1) or OFF (0). Humans use Denary (Base-10), but computers can only understand 1s and 0s.

💡 Key Fact: A bit is the smallest unit of data – a single 1 or 0. A byte is 8 bits.

🔢 The 8-Bit Place Value Grid

To convert between denary and binary, use the place value grid. Each column represents a power of 2:

128 64 32 16 8 4 2 1

Place a 1 in the columns you need to add up to your target number. Place a 0 in all other columns.

📝 Example: Convert 42 to Binary

42 = 32 + 8 + 2

So in binary: 00101010 (columns: 32, 8, 2)

🔄 Convert Binary to Denary

Add up the column values where there is a 1.

Example: 10110101 = 128 + 32 + 16 + 4 + 1 = 181

⭐ Extension Tasks

  • Challenge 1: Convert the denary number 255 into binary. What do you notice about all 8 bits?
  • Challenge 2: What is the largest denary number you can represent with 8 bits? What about 16 bits?
  • Challenge 3: Research: Why do computers use 8-bit bytes instead of 10-bit? Write a short paragraph explaining your findings.

🔁 Plenary – Binary Bingo

  • Choose 5 denary numbers between 0 and 255.
  • Write them down on a piece of paper.
  • Your teacher will call out binary numbers. Convert them to denary and cross them off!
  • First to cross off all 5 wins!
