1. Automated Systems

What is an automated system?

An automated system is a system that carries out a task or a series of tasks without the need for a human to be directly involved. Automated systems are used in many areas, including industry, transport, agriculture, medicine, and the home.

The three main components

Every automated system is made up of three key parts:

  • Sensors — detect changes in the environment (input). Examples: temperature sensor, light sensor, pressure sensor, motion sensor.
  • Microprocessor — processes the data from the sensors, compares it with stored values, and decides what to do (process).
  • Actuators — carry out an action (output), such as opening a valve, turning on a motor, or moving a robot arm.
📝 Exam tip: When describing an automated system, always mention the sensor, the microprocessor, and the actuator. Many marks are lost by students who only describe the sensor and the output.

How the feedback loop works

An automated system uses a feedback loop. The system:

  1. Senses a change in the environment using a sensor.
  2. Processes the data using a microprocessor and compares it with a stored value or threshold.
  3. Acts using an actuator if the value is outside the acceptable range.
  4. Checks again — the sensor takes a new reading, and the cycle repeats.

This lets the system keep working correctly without a person watching it.

Advantages and disadvantages of automated systems

Advantages:

  • Can work 24/7 without breaks or holidays.
  • More consistent and accurate than humans — no tiredness or human error.
  • Can work in dangerous or hazardous environments (e.g., deep sea, space, chemical plants).
  • Reduces the cost of paying human workers over time.

Disadvantages:

  • High initial cost of purchasing and installing the system.
  • Can lead to job losses for human workers.
  • If the system malfunctions, it may be dangerous or cause damage.
  • Less flexible than humans — may not cope with unexpected situations.
📝 Exam tip: If a question asks you to "discuss" or "evaluate" automated systems, give both advantages and disadvantages, and make a clear judgement at the end.

2. Robotics

What is a robot?

A robot is a machine that can carry out tasks automatically. Robots have three key characteristics:

  • They have a mechanical structure or framework.
  • They have electrical components such as sensors, microprocessors, and actuators.
  • They are programmable.

Roles of robots

Robots are used in many different areas:

  • Industry — manufacturing, assembly lines, packing products. Robots are precise, consistent, and can work without breaks.
  • Transport — self-driving cars, trains, and buses. Robots use sensors such as cameras, radar, and GPS to navigate.
  • Agriculture — sowing seeds, applying fertiliser, harvesting crops. Robots may use GPS for navigation and sensors to detect ripe fruit.
  • Medicine — surgical robots make precise incisions, remove tissue, and stitch patients. They are more accurate and do not have hand tremors like humans.
  • Domestic — robot vacuum cleaners, lawn mowers, and kitchen robots.
  • Entertainment — robot toys, robot servers, and interactive robots like Aibo.
📝 Exam tip: If a question asks you to describe how a robot works, always mention the sensors, the microprocessor, and the actuators — this is what distinguishes robots from simple machines.

Advantages and disadvantages of robots

Advantages:

  • Work faster than humans and without breaks.
  • More accurate and consistent — no human error or tiredness.
  • Can work in dangerous environments, reducing risks to humans.
  • Reduce the cost of human labour in the long term.

Disadvantages:

  • High cost of purchase and maintenance.
  • Can be unreliable — may not handle unexpected situations.
  • Job losses for human workers.
  • May not perform as well as a skilled human in some tasks.

  • Give two advantages and two disadvantages of using robots in industry.

3. Artificial Intelligence (AI)

What is AI?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a branch of computer science that deals with the simulation of intelligent behaviours by computers. AI systems can collect data, apply rules, reason, and learn.

Key characteristics of AI

  • Collection of data and rules — AI systems need data to work with. Rules are used to determine the answers to questions. They may be programmed by a human or discovered by machine learning.
  • Ability to reason — AI can use reasoning to find out new information based on the rules it is given. Example: Humans eat vegetables. Zach is human. Reasoning deduces: Zach eats vegetables.
  • Ability to learn and adapt — the system can improve its performance over time as it is exposed to more data.
📝 Exam tip: If asked to "describe the characteristics of AI," you must mention all three: data and rules, reasoning, and learning/adapting. Don't just say "it is intelligent."

Expert systems

An expert system is a computer program that simulates a human expert in a particular field. It is made up of four main components:

  • Knowledge base — stores facts about the domain (e.g., symptoms and diseases).
  • Rule base — stores rules on how the facts can be used. Rules are usually IF–THEN statements. Example: IF the patient has a fever AND a cough THEN the patient may have the flu.
  • Inference engine — controls the steps taken to solve the problem. It uses the rule base and knowledge base to deduce conclusions.
  • Interface — how the user interacts with the system (e.g., web page, chatbot, mobile app).

Machine learning

Machine learning is where a program is able to automatically adapt its own processes or data. Rather than giving the program rules, a large amount of training data is collected by the program, which then makes its own rules.

Example: A program is given many images of fish and chips. It looks at properties such as colour, shape, and texture. It finds patterns in the data and learns to tell the difference — without being told the rules for what makes a fish or a chip.

5. Exam Technique

Command words

Pay attention to the command word — it tells you how much detail is needed:

  • State / Give / Name — a short answer, often one word or phrase. No explanation needed.
  • Describe — say what happens or what something is. More detail than "state," but no need to explain why.
  • Explain — say how or why. You need to give reasons and make connections.
  • Compare — give similarities and differences.
  • Discuss / Evaluate — give advantages and disadvantages, and make a judgement.
📝 Mark allocation: Look at the number of marks. A 2-mark question usually needs two distinct points. A 3-mark question might need three points, or one point with three stages of development. Don't write one sentence for a 4-mark question — you won't get full marks.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Describing an automated system without mentioning the microprocessor.
  • Confusing sensors and actuators.
  • Saying a robot is "intelligent" without explaining how (sensors, rules, reasoning).
  • Describing an expert system without mentioning the knowledge base, rule base, or inference engine.
  • Saying machine learning uses pre-programmed rules (it creates its own rules from data).
  • Writing vague answers like "it is useful" instead of being specific.

Question 1

Describe the three main components of an automated system and explain the role of each. (6 marks)

Sensors — detect changes in the environment, such as temperature, light, or motion. They provide the input to the system.

Microprocessor — processes the data from the sensors, compares it with stored values or thresholds, and decides what action to take. It provides the processing stage.

Actuators — carry out an action based on the microprocessor's decision, such as opening a valve, turning on a motor, or moving a robot arm. They provide the output.

(6 marks: 2 for each component — 1 for naming it, 1 for explaining its role.)

Question 2

Explain what is meant by a feedback loop in an automated system and why it is important. (3 marks)

A feedback loop is where the system senses a change, processes it, acts on it, and then checks again with a new sensor reading.

It is important because it allows the system to keep working correctly without a person watching it — the system can continuously adjust itself to stay within acceptable limits.

(3 marks: 1 for describing the loop, 1 for the check/act cycle, 1 for the benefit of continuous adjustment without human involvement.)

Question 3

Describe two advantages and two disadvantages of using robots in manufacturing. (4 marks)

Advantages: Robots can work 24/7 without breaks or holidays; they are more consistent and accurate than humans, reducing errors.

Disadvantages: The initial cost of purchasing and installing robots is high; robots can cause job losses for human workers.

(4 marks: 1 for each advantage, 1 for each disadvantage.)

Question 4

Name the four components of an expert system and explain the role of the inference engine. (5 marks)

The four components are: knowledge base, rule base, inference engine, and interface.

The inference engine controls the steps taken to solve the problem. It uses the rule base and knowledge base to deduce conclusions — it applies the IF–THEN rules to the facts to reach an answer.

(5 marks: 4 for naming the components, 1 for explaining the inference engine's role.)

Question 5

Explain the difference between an expert system and machine learning, and give one example of each. (4 marks)

An expert system uses pre-programmed rules and a knowledge base to simulate a human expert. Example: a medical diagnosis system that uses IF–THEN rules to suggest a disease from symptoms.

Machine learning does not use pre-programmed rules — the program is given training data and creates its own rules by finding patterns. Example: a program that learns to tell fish from chips by analysing many labelled images.

(4 marks: 1 for expert system definition, 1 for expert system example, 1 for machine learning definition, 1 for machine learning example.)
