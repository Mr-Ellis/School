Profile

I am an experienced Computer Science teacher with sixteen years in the classroom across four international schools in three countries. I specialise in preparing students for IB and IGCSE exams, building curricula that are rigorous, engaging, and grounded in real-world application.

My teaching philosophy is built on structure and warmth. I create a classroom environment where students feel supported to take intellectual risks, while maintaining high expectations for academic rigour. I am constantly refining my practice through a fantastic online community of Computer Science educators, where we share insights and resources to stay at the forefront of the subject.

I am a builder of resources. I created and maintain a comprehensive website (mr-ellis.github.io/School) that houses my entire curriculum and serves as the primary resource for all my lessons. This library covers IB, IGCSE, and Key Stage 3, and I regularly share these materials with colleagues.

Beyond the classroom, I coordinate student entry into external competitions and run preparation sessions. I supervise Duke of Edinburgh students completing Python coursework, and oversee CAS students delivering a Blender and 3D printing course to younger pupils. Having recently completed the IB Category 2 training for the new curriculum, I am now looking to take the next step in my career by becoming an IB Examiner.

Employment History

ISR International School on the Rhine

Computer Science Teacher, Grades 7–12  ·  08/2020 – Present

I teach Computer Science across Grades 7–12, preparing students for Cambridge IGCSE and the IB Diploma Programme. My work is centred on curriculum alignment, strong computational thinking, and outcomes that meet or exceed global averages in both IB and Cambridge Computer Science.

In Grades 7 and 8, students follow the Cambridge Lower Secondary Computing syllabus — a solid foundation in programming, data representation, networks and digital systems that aligns closely with Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science (0478) and supports a smooth transition into Upper School.

My classroom practice is grounded in differentiation. I use tiered tasks, paired programming, adaptive learning platforms and targeted feedback so that students of all abilities make meaningful progress. Beyond the classroom, I lead enrichment through robotics workshops, AI clubs and hackathon preparation, and I organise industry-linked experiences such as visits to Infosys’ Strategic Innovation Center in Düsseldorf, where students explore AI, autonomous systems and immersive technologies.

I also supervise Duke of Edinburgh students completing Python coursework, and oversee CAS students delivering a Blender and 3D printing course to younger pupils. I coordinate student entry into external competitions — including the International Machine Learning Competition and the GV Hackathon — and run preparation sessions using the school’s Quarky robots and BBC micro:bits.

Secondary Computing / Computer Science Teacher  ·  08/2014 – 07/2020
