The "One Rule" Hackathon

Junior Challenge: Train an AI model with your Micro:bit, then build an interactive webpage that reacts to your movements.

The One Rule

"A first-time user, with no help from your team, has to understand your idea within two minutes of opening your project."

🤖 What is Micro:bit CreateAI?

CreateAI lets you record your own movement data (waving, shaking, tilting) using the Micro:bit's accelerometer. You train a machine learning model in the browser, then use that model in MakeCode to trigger actions—like controlling a webpage. No servos or extra hardware needed.

  • Equipment needed: Micro:bit V2, battery pack, computer with Bluetooth (or a second Micro:bit for radio link).
  • Workflow: Collect data → Train model → Test model → Code in MakeCode → Run on Micro:bit.
  • Your physical prototype: The Micro:bit itself becomes the sensor. You move it; the AI recognises the gesture; the webpage responds.

5 Brilliant CreateAI Ideas

These ideas use trained movement models to create a physical-to-digital interaction. You can pitch your own idea too—just make sure it follows the One Rule.

CreateAI

1. The "Air Drum" Kit

Train a model to recognise three distinct arm movements: a sharp downward hit (snare), a sideways hit (hi-hat), and a double tap (kick). The browser displays a drum kit that lights up and plays sounds when the AI recognises each gesture.

What you need: One Micro:bit, a battery pack, and your hands. No physical drums required.
The mechanism: Accelerometer gesture recognition (ML classification).
Tech: CreateAI + MakeCode + Web Audio
CreateAI

2. The "Secret Handshake" Lock

Explain how smart locks use pattern recognition. Train the Micro:bit to recognise a specific sequence of movements (e.g., shake twice, then tilt left). The webpage shows a vault door that only unlocks when the AI is highly confident the sequence is correct.

What you need: One Micro:bit, battery pack. Optional: a cardboard box as a "vault."
The mechanism: Sequence classification with certainty threshold.
Tech: CreateAI + Confidence Scores + Web Animation
CreateAI

3. The "Activity" Pet

Explain fitness trackers. Train the model to distinguish between "still," "walking," and "running." The browser shows a virtual pet that sleeps when still, walks when you walk, and runs when you run. This demonstrates how wearables classify activity.

What you need: One Micro:bit, battery pack, a wrist strap or pocket to hold it.
The mechanism: Activity classification from motion data.
Tech: CreateAI + MakeCode + Web CSS Animations
CreateAI

4. The "Muscle" Trainer

Explain how physical therapy apps track form. Train the model to recognise "good form" (smooth, controlled movement) vs. "bad form" (jerky or excessive movement). The browser shows a form score and a diagram of muscle engagement, updating in real time.

What you need: One Micro:bit, battery pack. Optional: a cardboard arm prop.
The mechanism: Binary classification (correct vs. incorrect movement).
Tech: CreateAI + Real-time Feedback + Web UI
CreateAI

5. The "Earthquake" Detector

Explain seismographs. Train the model to distinguish between "still," "gentle sway," and "sharp jolt." The browser shows a Richter scale and a building that shakes only when the AI recognises seismic-like movement. This simulates how real sensors classify ground motion.

What you need: One Micro:bit, battery pack. Optional: a cardboard box as a "building."
The mechanism: Vibration pattern classification.
Tech: CreateAI + MakeCode + Web Animation
