The "One Rule" Hackathon

Junior Challenge: Demystify everyday technology with a physical Micro:bit prototype and an interactive web demonstration.

The One Rule

"A first-time user, with no help from your team, has to understand your idea within two minutes of opening your project."

This Year's Challenge

1

Choose the Tech

Pick a technology people use every day without knowing how it works.

2

Find the Mechanism

Identify the core physical or logical part that actually does the work.

3

Build the Web Demo

Create a browser version where inputs change and outputs update instantly.

🏆 The "Judge's Choice" Standouts

If you are pitching to a panel of engineers, scientists, or experienced developers, they are looking for technical elegance, mechanism accuracy, and an "Aha!" moment. These three ideas have been highlighted because they perfectly balance simple hardware with deep, impressive concepts:

  • 1. The Refrigerator Cooling Cycle (The Physics Winner) Smart people know fridges don't "make cold"—they move heat. This project visualizes the invisible vapor-compression cycle. It takes a complex thermodynamic concept and makes it a tangible, step-by-step digital simulation.
  • 2. The "Pinch" Gas Stove (The Safety Interlock Winner) This uses CreateAI to train a model on a *sequence* of movements (push, then turn), which shows a higher level of AI sophistication than a single gesture. It perfectly demonstrates the "state machine" logic that prevents accidental gas leaks.
  • 3. The "Invisible" Barcode Scanner (The Hardware Hack Winner) This is the cleverest use of the physical Micro:bit. Using the LED matrix as a light sensor to read a barcode is a classic hardware hack. It bridges analog physics (light) and digital computing (binary code) right in front of the judges' eyes.

(Of course, you are not limited to these! Any of the 12 ideas below can win if executed perfectly.)

🛠️ Your Toolkit

Hardware: You only need the Micro:bit, a battery pack, jumper wires, aluminum foil, and cardboard. No servos or complex motors.

Software: You can use basic MakeCode (using built-in sensors like light, temp, and touch) OR you can use Micro:bit CreateAI to train models on physical gestures for a more advanced prototype.

12 Brilliant Ideas

🏆 Judge's Choice
Hardware Sensor

1. The "Invisible" Barcode Scanner

Everyday Tech: Grocery store checkout scanners.
The Mechanism: Light Reflection. Black bars absorb light; white gaps reflect it back to a sensor.
The Build: Print a barcode on paper. Use the Micro:bit's built-in light sensor (the LED grid). As the user slides the Micro:bit across the barcode, the changing light levels are sent to the browser. The webpage draws a "laser line" and translates the light/dark into binary "1"s and "0"s, eventually revealing the product price.
Tech: Light Sensor + Web Serial
CreateAI

2. The Zipper Interlock

Everyday Tech: A zipper on a jacket.
The Mechanism: Mechanical Interlocking. The slider forces two rows of teeth to interlock one by one.
The Build: Tape the Micro:bit to a cardboard "zipper pull." Train an AI model to recognize the smooth, continuous motion of pulling the zipper up vs. pulling it down. The webpage shows a giant 3D zipper. As the user physically pulls the cardboard prop, the digital zipper teeth interlock or separate in perfect sync.
Tech: CreateAI Motion + Web CSS 3D
🏆 Judge's Choice
Hardware Sensor

3. The Refrigerator Cooling Cycle

Everyday Tech: A kitchen fridge.
The Mechanism: Phase Change (Compression & Expansion). Gas absorbs heat when it expands and releases heat when compressed.
The Build: Use the Micro:bit's temperature sensor. Pinch the sensor to simulate heat. The webpage shows a cutaway of a fridge. As the temperature rises, the user clicks a "Compress" button on the web page (or presses the Micro:bit button). The webpage animates the gas turning from blue (cold) to red (hot), showing heat being pushed out of the back coils.
Tech: Temp Sensor + Web Animation
Hardware Sensor

4. The "Ghost" Touchscreen

Everyday Tech: Smartphone screens.
The Mechanism: Capacitance. Your body holds an electrical charge that disrupts the screen's electric field.
The Build: Attach a jumper wire to Pin 1 and tape a piece of aluminum foil to it. When the user touches the foil, it sends a signal. The webpage shows a giant phone screen. The moment the foil is touched, a glowing "fingerprint" ripple expands from the contact point, opening a hidden app.
Tech: Touch Pin + Web Animation
CreateAI

5. The "Wrist-Raise" Wake Screen

Everyday Tech: Smartwatches.
The Mechanism: Wake-on-Raise Sensors. Saving battery by only turning on the screen when you look at it.
The Build: Tape the Micro:bit to a cardboard watch strap. Train an AI model to recognize "Arm Down" vs. "Arm Raised." The webpage shows a giant watch face. When the user physically raises their arm, the AI detects the gesture, and the digital watch screen lights up with the time.
Tech: CreateAI Classification + Web UI
🏆 Judge's Choice
CreateAI

6. The "Pinch" Gas Stove

Everyday Tech: A gas stove knob.
The Mechanism: Push-to-Turn Safety. You must push the knob down before you can turn it, preventing accidental gas leaks.
The Build: Tape the Micro:bit to a cardboard "stove knob." Train an AI model to recognize the "Press Down" gesture vs. a "Twist" gesture. The webpage shows a stove burner. If the user just twists the prop, the screen flashes "LOCKED - PUSH FIRST!" If they push down and then twist, the AI recognizes the sequence, and the digital flame ignites.
Tech: CreateAI Sequence + Web Animation
CreateAI

7. The "Air Gesture" Thermostat

Everyday Tech: Smart home thermostats.
The Mechanism: Gesture Control. Modern smart homes let you wave your hand to adjust temperature without touching a dial.
The Build: Train a model to recognize "Swipe Up" (increase temp) and "Swipe Down" (decrease). The webpage shows a digital thermostat. As you swipe the physical Micro:bit in the air, the digital number climbs or falls, and the background color shifts from blue (cold) to red (hot).
Tech: CreateAI Directional Gestures + Web UI
Hardware Sensor

8. Motion Security Light

Everyday Tech: Automatic porch lights.
The Mechanism: PIR (Passive Infrared) Sensors. Detecting the body heat of a moving person.
The Build: Use the Micro:bit's accelerometer or light sensor as a proxy for motion. Wave your hand over the board. The webpage shows a dark, spooky house. When the sensor detects "motion," the porch light flickers on, and a "MOTION DETECTED" alert flashes on screen.
Tech: Accelerometer/Light + Web Animation
CreateAI

9. The "Secret Handshake" Lock

Everyday Tech: Smart door locks.
The Mechanism: Pattern Recognition. Smart locks look for a specific, secure sequence of movements.
The Build: Train an AI model to recognize a unique sequence (e.g., shake, pause, tilt left). The webpage shows a massive vault door. The vault only unlocks when the AI is highly confident the exact sequence was performed. A random shake results in an "Access Denied" error.
Tech: CreateAI Sequence + Web Animation
Hardware Sensor

10. The "Smart" Plant Sensor

Everyday Tech: Self-watering planters / Soil moisture meters.
The Mechanism: Electrical Conductivity. Wet soil conducts electricity better than dry soil.
The Build: Attach jumper wires to the Micro:bit pins and stick them into a cup of soil. Add water. The webpage shows a digital plant. As the soil goes from dry to wet, the webpage's "hydration bar" fills up, and the plant perks up from a wilted state to a bright green, happy state.
Tech: Touch Pins (Conductivity) + Web UI
CreateAI

11. The "Unbalanced" Washing Machine

Everyday Tech: A front-loading washing machine.
The Mechanism: Vibration Sensing. The machine detects if the load is uneven and stops to prevent damage.
The Build: Train an AI model to recognize "Smooth Spin" vs. "Heavy Thumping." The webpage shows a spinning washing machine drum. When the user shakes the Micro:bit heavily (simulating an unbalanced load), the AI detects the thumping, the digital drum starts to wobble violently, and a "PAUSED - UNBALANCED LOAD" error flashes on the screen.
Tech: CreateAI Vibration + Web CSS
CreateAI

12. The "Tilt" Game Controller

Everyday Tech: Mobile game controllers / Wii Remotes.
The Mechanism: Accelerometers. Converting physical tilt into digital movement.
The Build: Train a model to recognize "Tilt Left," "Tilt Right," and "Flat." Build a cardboard controller shell for the Micro:bit. The webpage runs a simple maze or endless-runner game. The player physically tilts the controller to steer the character and avoid obstacles on the screen.
Tech: CreateAI Continuous Steering + Web Game Logic
