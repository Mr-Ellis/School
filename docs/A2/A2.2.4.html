A2.2.4 – Explain the Concepts and Applications of Network Segmentation

📌 Learning Objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain what network segmentation is and why it is used
  • Explain the concept of a VLAN (Virtual Local Area Network)
  • Identify the benefits of segmentation (security, performance, broadcast control)
  • Apply segmentation concepts to real-world scenarios

🎯 IB Command Term: Explain

Explain – “Give a detailed account including reasons or causes.”

Naming a VLAN is not explaining it. You must be able to explain what problem segmentation solves, how it works, and what the consequences are for security and performance.

🔑 Key Terms

TermDefinition
Network SegmentationThe practice of splitting a large network into smaller, isolated sub-networks (segments).
VLANVirtual Local Area Network. A logical grouping of devices on a network, regardless of their physical location. Devices in one VLAN act as if they are on a separate physical network.
Broadcast DomainA logical division of a network where all devices receive broadcast traffic. Segmentation reduces the size of broadcast domains.
SubnetA logical subdivision of an IP network. Used to separate networks at the IP layer.
DMZDemilitarised Zone. A physical or logical sub-network that contains external-facing services to an untrusted network (like the internet).

💡 The Big Idea: Divide and Protect

In a flat network (where every device is connected to every other device), a single compromised computer can spread malware to the entire network. Segmentation solves this by creating "walls" between different parts of the network.

Think of it like a ship: a single hull breach could sink the whole vessel. But if the ship is divided into watertight compartments (segments), a breach in one compartment only floods that section—the ship stays afloat.

The one rule you must never break

Segmentation is not just about security. It also improves performance. When you split a network, you reduce the amount of broadcast traffic (data sent to every device). This means devices spend less time processing irrelevant data and more time doing useful work.

🧠 Activity 3: Exam-Style Analysis (Advanced)

Read the scenario below and write a short answer. Click "Show Model Answer" to compare your response.

Scenario

A company has one flat network with 500 employees. Recently, a virus infected one employee's laptop and spread to the company's customer database within minutes. The IT manager wants to implement VLANs to prevent this from happening again.

Explain how network segmentation using VLANs would have limited the damage of this attack. (4 marks)

Model Answer:
If the company had used VLANs, the employee's laptop would be in a separate VLAN (e.g., a general staff VLAN) to the customer database (e.g., a secure server VLAN). When the virus infected the laptop, it would only be able to spread to other devices within that same VLAN. The VLAN boundaries would act as a firewall, preventing the virus from reaching the customer database VLAN. This limits the spread of the attack, protects critical data, and gives the IT team time to isolate the infected machine.
