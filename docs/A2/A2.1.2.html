🔑 Key Terms (Simplified)

InfrastructureWhat it isKey BenefitKey Limitation
InternetThe global network of networks that connects everything.Global connectivity and access to information.Depends on network availability; security risks.
Cloud ComputingUsing someone else's powerful computers over the internet (e.g., Google Drive, AWS).Scalable resources; no need to buy local hardware.Needs internet; less direct control; privacy concerns.
Distributed SystemsMany computers working together to act like one powerful system.Improved reliability; can handle huge tasks.Complex to coordinate; network failures can affect it.
Edge ComputingProcessing data right where it is created, instead of sending it to a distant data centre.Very low latency; saves bandwidth.More devices to manage; limited local processing power.
Mobile NetworksWireless infrastructure (4G/5G) that connects devices on the move.Mobility and wide coverage.Signal varies; congestion can slow speeds.

📖 Case Study: Rescue Robots & Disaster Recovery

In the 2024 IB Computer Science case study, BotPro designs rescue robots to search collapsed buildings after earthquakes or explosions. These robots must navigate debris, identify survivors, and operate in environments where the internet or GPS may be unavailable.

Why Edge Computing is Essential

Rescue robots use edge computing because they cannot rely on a distant cloud. If a robot sends camera footage to a data centre to decide whether a shape is a survivor or rubble, the delay (latency) could cost lives. By processing data locally on the robot, it can make split-second decisions even when network connectivity is poor or completely lost[reference:1].

Connecting to Disaster Recovery

Disaster recovery is not just about backing up data; it is about keeping critical systems running when infrastructure fails. Edge computing provides resilience: if the central network goes down, edge devices (like rescue robots, or local emergency servers) can continue to operate independently. This is why modern disaster recovery plans increasingly include edge computing as a way to maintain functionality during outages[reference:2].

📌 EXAM LINK: If a question asks how edge computing supports disaster recovery, focus on local processing (works without internet), low latency (fast decisions), and resilience (continues operating when central systems fail).
