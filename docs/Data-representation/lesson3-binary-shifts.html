Binary Shifts & Two's Complement
Lesson 3 · Logical shifts, multiplication, division and negative numbers

Learning Objectives

  • Perform logical binary shifts on 8-bit binary integers (left and right)
  • Understand the effect of shifts: multiplication, division and loss of precision
  • Use two's complement to represent positive and negative 8-bit integers
💡 Key Fact: A left shift multiplies by 2; a right shift divides by 2. Bits shifted off the end are lost, which can cause loss of precision.

⬅️➡️ Logical Binary Shifts

A binary shift moves all bits left or right by a specified number of places. Vacated spaces are filled with zeros.

00111100 → 01111000

Left Shift by 1 (multiplies by 2)

01011010 → 00101101

Right Shift by 1 (divides by 2)

⚠️ Loss of Precision

When a right shift is performed, the least significant bit (rightmost) may be lost. This results in rounding down.

  • Example: 45 (00101101) right shift by 1 → 22 (00010110)
  • The fractional part (.5) is lost because binary registers cannot store decimal places.

➗ Two's Complement

Two's complement is used to represent negative integers in binary.

Method:

  1. Write the positive binary number
  2. Invert all bits (0→1, 1→0)
  3. Add 1 to the result

Example: -15 in 8-bit two's complement

  • 15 = 00001111
  • Invert: 11110000
  • Add 1: 11110001 → -15
