🎯 Starter Activity – What is Currency?

We use it every day, but what actually is currency? Discuss with a partner: what makes something work as money?

💡 Currencies generally have these properties:
  • Fungible — a medium of exchange, one unit is interchangeable with another
  • Durable — it can be reused many times
  • Portable — easily carried around
  • Recognisable — other people understand its value
  • Stable — its value stays fairly constant over time

Traditional currencies are usually created by a country's government and accepted as payment for taxes and debts. But is a £10 note worth £10 because of the paper? No — currency itself has no intrinsic value. Its value comes from the faith that people have in it.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand the concept of a digital currency and how digital currencies are used
  • Understand the process of blockchain and how it is used to track digital currency transactions
💡 Key Fact: Digital currencies (cryptocurrencies) exist only electronically. Like ordinary currencies, they have no intrinsic value — their worth is built on what people believe they are worth.

📉 Problems with Traditional Currency

Why were cryptocurrencies invented? Because ordinary currencies have weaknesses:

ProblemWhat it means
Counterfeiting Fake coins and notes can be created.
Hyperinflation Governments or central banks can print too much money, making each unit worth less. Prices rise rapidly — sometimes daily.
Commission fees Currency exchange and international transfers often carry high fees.
Slow transfers International bank transfers can take days.
⚠️ Hyperinflation: Argentina (1975–1990) saw average annual inflation of around 300%. Yugoslavia's peaked at more than 100 trillion percent per year. Prices rose so quickly that people spent money as fast as they received it.

Traditional currencies are also backed by something — for example, gold stored in central banks, foreign currency reserves, or Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) issued by the IMF. Cryptocurrencies typically have nothing backing them.

🔑 How Digital Currencies Work

Cryptocurrencies move between users using cryptography. When you create a cryptocurrency account, you generate two digital keys:

KeyPurposeAnalogy
Public key Used to receive money. Contains the wallet's digital currency address. Can be shared freely. Like an account number / sort code
Private key Used to spend or transfer money. Must be kept secret. Like a PIN on a bank card

Where do you keep them?

  • In a file or simple database called a wallet.
  • If the file with the keys is printed so it can't be digitally hacked, this is called cold storage.

Spending digital currency

To send currency you provide:

  1. The public key (which contains the destination address)
  2. An electronic signature created using your private key

Everyone on the network can then verify the transaction as valid — proving that the person spending the money actually owns it.

⛓️ The Blockchain

The blockchain is the most revolutionary part of digital currencies. It is a distributed digital ledger — a public record of every transaction ever made.

What is in each block?

  • The two parties involved in the transaction (sender and receiver)
  • A timestamp of when the transaction occurred
  • The amount of currency transferred
  • Its own hash (a unique identifier)
  • A reference to the previous block's hash

Why "distributed"?

  • The ledger is public — anyone can download and view it.
  • Because it's distributed, there are many copies — there is no central authority.
  • If one copy is altered, it will disagree with all the other copies on the network, and the alteration will be spotted immediately.

How it prevents tampering

Each block links back to the previous block via its hash. This creates a chain. If you try to change anything in an earlier block, its hash changes — and every following block's "previous hash" reference becomes wrong. The whole chain visibly breaks, so fraud is easy to detect.

📝 Beyond currency: The blockchain concept is also used for other purposes — for example, NFTs (non-fungible tokens) use blockchains to prove ownership of digital items.

💡 Uses & Benefits of Digital Currency

Uses

  • International transfers — take minutes instead of days
  • Paying for goods and services — anywhere that accepts the currency
  • Saving — some people use it as a savings account

Benefits

  • Hard to counterfeit (individual accounts may still be hacked)
  • Send money anywhere in the world with very low fees — no bank needed
  • The amount of currency is tightly controlled by the protocol — no government can just print more

Is hyperinflation prevented?

The supply of most cryptocurrencies is heavily controlled, so printing-driven hyperinflation isn't possible. However, if people lose trust in the currency, no one will want it — and its value can fall to zero. That's effectively the same outcome as hyperinflation.

⚠️ Price volatility: Because cryptocurrencies have no intrinsic value, prices can be extremely volatile. Bitcoin, for example, has lost or gained more than half its value within a single year.

Lesson Overview

Students are introduced to digital currencies (cryptocurrencies). The lesson begins with what currency is and the problems with traditional currencies (counterfeiting, hyperinflation, fees), which explains why cryptocurrencies were created. Students then learn how keys, wallets, and the blockchain fit together, and finish with a real exploration of the bitcoin blockchain explorer.

Timing

  • 0–5 min — Starter: What is currency? Properties discussion
  • 5–15 min — Problems with currency: hyperinflation, counterfeiting, fees
  • 15–25 min — Public/private keys and wallets; Worksheet 7 Tasks 1 & 2
  • 25–35 min — The blockchain: structure, distribution, tamper-resistance
  • 35–50 min — Blockchain Explorer: Genesis Block and Genesis Address; Worksheet 7 Task 3
  • 50–60 min — Uses and benefits; plenary match-the-definition game; hand out Homework 7

Key Teaching Points

  • Currency has no intrinsic value — its value is built on faith. This is true of both traditional and digital currencies.
  • The public key is like an account number (share freely); the private key is like a PIN (keep secret).
  • The blockchain is distributed — no central authority. Changing one copy would disagree with all the others, so fraud is instantly detected.
  • Blockchains are not just for currency — NFTs and other digital ownership schemes use them too.
  • Common misconception: students may think bitcoin is "anonymous". It's actually pseudonymous — every transaction is public and permanent.

Differentiation

  • Support: Use the blockchain lab demo to make the "chain breaks if altered" idea tangible before asking for written explanations.
  • Stretch: Ask students to research proof-of-work and evaluate its environmental impact.
  • EAL / Dyslexia: The blockchain lab and the real explorer pages provide concrete, visual anchors for abstract concepts.

Assessment

  • Formative: blockchain lab observations, match-the-definition score.
  • Summative: Worksheet 7 submission on Schoolbox.
  • Retrieval: Homework 7 (Chinara scenario).
