Lesson 4 · Web Technologies & Cookies
Year 10 · Networks · Web Technologies, Cookies & Privacy

Learning Objectives

  • Describe what a cookie is and where it is stored
  • Explain the purpose of cookies and how they improve the user experience
  • Distinguish between session cookies, persistent cookies, and third-party cookies
  • Understand the privacy implications of cookies and how users can control them
  • Describe other web technologies used in modern websites (JavaScript, HTTPS, CDNs)
💡 Key Fact: A cookie is a small text file stored on your device by a website. It lets the website remember information about you — like your login status, shopping basket, or language preference — between page loads and visits.
🎯 Starter Activity – Think-Pair-Share

Think: Have you ever noticed that when you return to a website, you're still logged in, or your shopping basket still has items in it? How do you think the website remembers that? Share your ideas with a partner.

Click to reveal discussion points
  • Websites store small pieces of data on your device called cookies.
  • Cookies act like a memory — they remember who you are and what you did.
  • Without cookies, websites would treat you like a brand new visitor every time you clicked a link.
  • Cookies are also used for tracking and advertising — which is why you see privacy banners on most websites.

🍪 What is a Cookie?

📌 Definition

A cookie is a small text file (usually under 4KB) that a website stores on your device when you visit it.

It contains a name, a value, and metadata like an expiry date.

📍 Where are they stored?

Cookies are stored locally in your web browser — not on the website's server.

You can view and delete them in your browser's settings under "Privacy & Security".

🔄 How do they work?

Every time you request a page from the same website, your browser sends the cookie back with the request.

This lets the server recognise you and personalise the page.

🧠 Remember: Cookies are per-site — a cookie created by amazon.co.uk is only sent back to amazon.co.uk, not to google.com or any other site.

🗂️ Types of Cookies

⏱️ Session Cookies

Temporary cookies that exist only while you're on the site. They're deleted automatically when you close your browser.

Example: Keeping you logged in while you browse.

📅 Persistent Cookies

Stored on your device for a set period of time (days, months, or years). They survive closing the browser.

Example: "Remember me" login, saved preferences.

👥 Third-Party Cookies

Created by a different domain than the one you're visiting — usually advertisers or analytics trackers.

Example: Ads following you around the web.

🎯 What Are Cookies Used For?

🔐 Authentication

Keeping you logged in as you move between pages.

🛒 Shopping Carts

Remembering the items you've added, even if you close the tab.

⚙️ Preferences

Storing your language, theme, or region settings.

📊 Analytics

Tracking which pages are popular so sites can improve.

🎯 Targeted Advertising

Showing ads based on what you've looked at elsewhere.

🛡️ Security

Detecting suspicious login attempts or fraudulent activity.

🔒 Privacy Concerns

While cookies make the web more convenient, they also raise privacy issues:

  • Tracking across sites — third-party cookies can build a detailed profile of your browsing habits.
  • Targeted advertising — ads that follow you around can feel intrusive.
  • Data selling — some companies sell cookie data to third parties.
  • Security risks — if cookies aren't encrypted, attackers could steal session data.
⚖️ The Law: In the UK and EU, websites must ask for your consent before setting non-essential cookies (GDPR). That's why you see "Accept all / Manage preferences" banners.

🛠️ Managing Cookies

🌐 Browser Settings

All modern browsers let you view, delete, and block cookies in their privacy settings.

🕵️ Incognito / Private Mode

Cookies created in private browsing are deleted when you close the window.

🧹 Cookie Cleaners

Extensions and tools that automatically clear cookies after each session.

🌐 Other Web Technologies

⚡ JavaScript

Runs in the browser to make pages interactive — animations, form validation, live updates.

🎨 CSS

Cascading Style Sheets control the layout, colours, and fonts of a web page.

🔐 HTTPS / TLS

Encrypts data between browser and server. Essential for any site that handles logins or payments.

🌍 CDN (Content Delivery Network)

A network of servers around the world that deliver content from the closest location to the user — faster loading.

📱 Responsive Design

Websites that adapt their layout to fit any screen size — phone, tablet, or desktop.

🗄️ Server-Side Scripting

Code like PHP, Python, or Node.js that runs on the web server to generate dynamic pages.

🔁 Plenary – Web Technologies & Cookies

Discuss the answers to the following questions with a partner:

  1. What is a cookie and where is it stored?
  2. Give three examples of how cookies are used on websites you visit.
  3. What is the difference between a session cookie and a persistent cookie?
  4. Why do websites have to ask for your consent before setting cookies?
Click to reveal suggested answers
  • Cookie: A small text file stored locally by the browser on your device. It contains a name and value that the website can read on future visits.
  • Uses: Keeping you logged in, remembering shopping cart items, saving preferences, tracking analytics, targeting ads, detecting fraud.
  • Session vs persistent: Session cookies are deleted when the browser closes. Persistent cookies remain on the device for a set time (days, months, years).
  • Consent: Under GDPR, websites must get the user's permission before storing non-essential cookies like those used for ads or analytics.
