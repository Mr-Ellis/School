Competitions
Build your CV, test your skills, and compete globally

Why compete?

University admissions officers see thousands of identical IB profiles. Competition experience is one of the strongest differentiators you can add to your CV. It shows initiative, computational thinking, and the ability to perform under pressure — qualities that top universities actively look for.

And the best part: you do not have to compete alone. I have physical hardware available — Quarky robots and micro:bits — to help you build, experiment, and prepare. Whether you are interested in algorithms, hardware, or both, there is a competition for you.

Open Competitions

International Machine Learning Competition (IMLC)

Qualification Round: Sep 2026 Online Individual

The IMLC is a global competition that challenges students to solve problems using computational thinking, algorithms, and problem-solving under time pressure. The qualification round is a written paper with multiple problems of increasing difficulty, designed to test how you decompose complex problems and design efficient solutions.

Why it matters: A strong result in the qualification round directly demonstrates algorithmic thinking and problem decomposition — skills that are assessed in IB Computer Science and valued by university admissions.

Preparation: I have past qualification papers and worked solutions available. We can run practice sessions during lunch or after school, and the problems are excellent preparation for Paper 2.

GV Hackathon

Annual Team-based Sprint format

A hackathon is a time-boxed sprint where teams design and build a solution to a real-world problem. You will brainstorm, prototype, and pitch your idea to judges. The focus is not just on code — it is on creativity, teamwork, and communication.

Why it matters: Hackathons develop exactly the skills universities want to see: collaboration, rapid prototyping, and the ability to present technical ideas to a non-technical audience. They are also genuinely fun.

Hardware advantage: You do not need to build a web app. With the Quarky robots and micro:bits available, your team can prototype physical solutions — sensors, robots, interactive devices — which makes your pitch stand out.

Hardware available to you

These are not toys. They are the same tools used in university robotics labs and industry prototyping. I have them available for competition preparation and project work.

Quarky Robot Kits

AI and robotics platform with camera, sensors, motors, and block/Python programming. Used for self-driving car projects, face recognition, and interactive builds. Ideal for hackathon prototyping and machine learning demonstrations.

BBC micro:bit

Pocket-sized programmable computer with LED display, buttons, accelerometer, compass, radio, and (on v2) a microphone and speaker. Perfect for sensor-based projects, wearable tech, and rapid prototyping of interactive devices.

What you get out of it

CV and university applications

Competition participation and results are concrete evidence of initiative and technical skill. They make your personal statement and reference letters stronger.

Problem-solving under pressure

Competitions force you to think algorithmically and debug quickly. These are the same skills you need for Paper 2 and for university interviews.

Teamwork and communication

Hackathons especially require explaining technical ideas to non-technical judges. This is a skill you will use for the rest of your career.

Something to aim for

Preparation has a purpose. You will learn more in a focused competition sprint than in months of passive study.
