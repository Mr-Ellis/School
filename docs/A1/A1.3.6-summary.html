💡 What Is a Control System?

A control system is a system designed to regulate or direct the behaviour of other devices or systems. They are fundamental in automating and regulating processes across a wide range of industries — from manufacturing to robotics to environmental control.

At the core of every control system are components that work together to:

  • Manage inputs — the desired condition or target
  • Process data — comparing desired vs actual
  • Generate outputs — the action taken to correct the system

Control systems rely on a precise feedback mechanism to ensure the process remains stable and meets its objectives.

📊 The Four Core Components — Click to Explore

Each component plays a specific role in the control loop. Click any card to see what it does.

🧠Controller
The "brain" of the system
The central component that governs operation by processing inputs and generating outputs. It implements the control algorithm — making decisions based on input data and feedback.

It compares the desired value (input) with the actual value (feedback) and determines the necessary action to minimise the difference, or error.

Examples: from simple thermostats to complex microprocessors in industrial automation.
👁️Sensors
The "eyes and ears" of the system
Devices that detect and measure physical quantities from the environment or the system itself — temperature, pressure, speed, light.

These measurements are converted into electrical signals that the controller can interpret.

The accuracy and reliability of sensors directly affects the control system's performance, because they provide the critical feedback for adjustment.

Examples: temperature sensor, humidity sensor, soil moisture sensor, proximity sensor, gas sensor.
💪Actuators
The "muscles" of the system
Components that carry out the physical actions or adjustments in response to commands from the controller.

They convert the controller's electrical signals into mechanical motion or other forms of energy — turning a valve, moving a robotic arm, adjusting a motor's speed.

Performance and precision are critical in applications requiring exact control — manufacturing equipment, robotics.
🔄Transducers
The energy converters
Devices that convert one form of energy into another. They bridge the gap between sensors, actuators and the controller.

A sensor may not directly provide the type of signal the controller can process. A transducer converts signals into a compatible form.

Example: a pressure sensor detects mechanical pressure and converts it into an electrical signal the controller can interpret. An actuator may need a specific voltage supplied by a transducer.

Transducers ensure all parts of the control system communicate effectively.

🔀 Open-Loop vs Closed-Loop

The distinction comes down to one thing: is there feedback? Click each card to explore.

Open-Loop Tap
No feedback from output back to input.
The system operates solely based on the initial input — no correction or adjustment based on the actual output.

Simple and predictable. Used when the relationship between input and output is straightforward.

Examples: basic timers, simple toaster (it heats for a fixed time regardless of how toasted the bread actually is).
Tap to reveal detail
Closed-Loop Tap
Continuously monitors the output and feeds it back.
Also called a feedback control system. If the output deviates from the desired input, the system makes corrections in real time to bring the output back in line with the target.

Higher accuracy and adaptability. Essential for applications where conditions change.

Examples: HVAC temperature control, thermostats, automatic elevator control, autonomous vehicles.

⚙️ The Control Algorithm

The control algorithm is the set of rules or mathematical procedures the controller uses to determine the appropriate output based on input and feedback.

It is the logic that drives the decision-making process within the controller. Algorithms vary in complexity:

TypeHow it works
Proportional controlOutput is adjusted in direct proportion to the error. Simple but can leave a small steady-state error.
PID controlProportional-Integral-Derivative — considers past, present and future errors to make precise adjustments. More complex but much more accurate.

The choice of control algorithm depends on the system's requirements: desired accuracy, speed of response, and stability.

🔍 Scenario Explorer — Identify the Components

For each real-world system, click the question to reveal its components.

🌡️ Home thermostat
  • Input: Desired temperature (setpoint) — e.g. 20°C.
  • Sensor: Temperature sensor — continuously monitors room temperature.
  • Controller: The thermostat — compares actual vs desired and decides.
  • Actuator: Heater or AC — heats or cools the room.
  • Process: Room temperature.
  • Feedback: Sensor continuously feeds temperature data back.
  • Type: Closed-loop.
🛗 Automatic elevator
  • Input: Floor requests, door status, position.
  • Sensors: Position sensors, floor sensors, door sensors.
  • Controller: Elevator control computer.
  • Actuators: Motor (to move), door mechanism (to open/close).
  • Feedback: Continuous sensor data on position and speed, so the elevator stops precisely.
  • Type: Closed-loop.
💧 Irrigation control system
  • Input: Desired soil moisture level.
  • Sensors: Soil moisture sensors, weather sensors.
  • Controller: Irrigation control unit.
  • Actuators: Water valves, pumps.
  • Feedback: Continuous moisture readings — the system shuts off once the target moisture is reached.
  • Type: Closed-loop.
🚦 Traffic signal control
  • Input: Desired traffic flow patterns, pedestrian requests.
  • Sensors: Vehicle detectors (induction loops, cameras), pedestrian buttons.
  • Controller: Traffic management computer.
  • Actuators: Traffic lights (red, amber, green).
  • Feedback: Sensor data on traffic volume, used to adjust timings in real time.
  • Type: Closed-loop.
🍞 Simple toaster
  • Input: Time setting (e.g. 3 minutes).
  • Controller: Timer mechanism.
  • Actuator: Heating element.
  • Sensor: None for controlling the toast.
  • Feedback: None — the toaster runs for the set time regardless of how toasted the bread actually is.
  • Type: Open-loop.
