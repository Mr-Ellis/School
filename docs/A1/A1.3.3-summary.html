A1.3.3 – Compare Different Approaches to Scheduling

1

Starter — Know Your Command Term 5 min

📖 Open Command Terms
Compare — "Give an account of the similarities and differences between two or more items, referring to both throughout."
2

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the four scheduling algorithms: FCFS, Round Robin, Priority, and Multilevel Queue.
  • Compare them across key dimensions — pre-emptive status, response time, fairness, overhead, and starvation risk.
  • Justify which algorithm is best for a given workload, using comparative language.

🏢 Operating System Recruitment Challenge

Microsoft is hiring a new CPU scheduler.

Four scheduling teams are competing. Your team must prove your algorithm deserves to run Windows 2030.

Here's how it works:

  • Poster Designer (Role A): Presents what your algorithm does — how it works, its slogan, one strength, one weakness, and where it's used.
  • Note Taker (Role B): Listens to every poster presentation, then makes the comparative case — explaining why Microsoft should choose your algorithm over the competition.

Microsoft already knows what each algorithm does. The contract goes to the team that makes the strongest comparative case.

3

Task Instructions 10 + 18 min

📋 Part 1 — Prepare Your Pitch (10 minutes)

Work in pairs. Each pair is assigned one scheduling algorithm.

👤 Role A — Poster Designer

  • Read your algorithm's Information Sheet.
  • Create a poster that shows:
    • How your algorithm works
    • A slogan
    • One strength and one weakness
    • Where it would be used in the real world
  • You will present this poster in the corner of the room.

👤 Role B — Note Taker & Challenger

  • Read your algorithm's Information Sheet.
  • Take detailed notes on:
    • Key facts (pre-emptive, response time, fairness, overhead, starvation risk)
    • How your algorithm compares to the other three

📌 Your Information Sheet

🗣️ Part 2 — The Pitch & Challenge (18 minutes)

8 min
Poster Presentations: Students with posters stand in the four corners of the room (one corner per algorithm). Each poster designer gives a 2-minute explanation of their algorithm to the class.
8 min
The Challenge: The note-takers (Role B) come forward. They must compare their algorithm against another team's and explain why Microsoft should choose theirs. Use comparative language: "Unlike FCFS…", "Whereas Round Robin…", "Both Priority and MLQ…"
2 min
The Decision: Sit down as a class. Vote on which algorithm won the contract. The winning team must have made the strongest comparative case — not just described their algorithm.
Rule for the Challenge: You may not describe your algorithm in isolation. Every argument must reference at least one other algorithm — "unlike FCFS…", "whereas Round Robin…". This is the Compare command term in action.

5

Task 4 — Starter Activity Questions if Jojo unavailable

Answer these questions in your book. Use your Information Sheet to help you.

❓ Is Round Robin always fair?

  • No. It's fair in CPU time, but it pays for that fairness with context-switching overhead.
  • The smaller the quantum, the fairer it feels — but the more time is wasted switching.
  • Compare: FCFS has almost no overhead, but it's not fair at all.

❓ Why is FCFS so simple? What's the downside?

  • Simple: No decisions to make — just a FIFO queue.
  • Downside: The convoy effect. One long job blocks every short job behind it.
  • Compare: Round Robin would pre-empt that long job, so short jobs get a turn.

❓ Hospital system — which algorithm?

  • Priority with ageing.
  • A life-critical process must jump the queue.
  • Compare: Round Robin is the wrong goal here — fairness would delay a critical alert behind a background log write.

❓ Which algorithm can starve processes?

  • Priority (without ageing) and Multilevel Queue (lower queues).
  • FCFS and Round Robin have no starvation risk.
  • Compare: Priority serves urgent work first, but starves low-priority work. FCFS never starves anyone — but it can make everyone wait.
6

Plenary 5 min

Launch Quizlet Launch Blooket
💡 Key Takeaway: "No scheduling approach is best in every way. FCFS is simple but non-pre-emptive and suffers the convoy effect; Round Robin is pre-emptive and responsive but pays in context-switching overhead; Priority serves urgent work first but can starve low-priority processes; Multilevel Queue separates workloads cleanly but can starve lower queues. To compare them is to say which is more responsive, which carries more overhead, which can starve — and to justify your choice for the workload in front of you."
