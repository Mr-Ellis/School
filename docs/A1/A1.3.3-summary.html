A1.3.3 – Compare Different Approaches to Scheduling

1

Starter — Know Your Command Term 5 min

📖 Open Command Terms
Compare – "Give an account of the similarities and differences between two or more items, referring to both throughout."
2

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the four scheduling algorithms: FCFS, Round Robin, Priority, and Multilevel Queue.
  • Compare them across key dimensions — pre-emptive status, response time, fairness, overhead, and starvation risk.
  • Justify which algorithm is best for a given workload, using comparative language.

🏢 Operating System Recruitment Challenge

Microsoft is hiring a new CPU scheduler.

Four scheduling teams are competing. Your team must prove your algorithm deserves to run Windows 2030.

The team that builds the strongest comparative case wins the contract.

3

Task 1 — The Pitch & Challenge 10 + 18 min

📋 Part 1 — Prepare Your Pitch (10 minutes)

Work in pairs. Each pair is assigned one scheduling algorithm.

👤 Role A — Poster Designer

  • Read your algorithm's Information Sheet.
  • Create a poster that shows:
    • How your algorithm works

    • One strength and one weakness
    • Where it would be used in the real world

👤 Role B — Note Taker & Challenger

  • Read your algorithm's Information Sheet.
  • Take detailed notes on:
    • Key facts (pre-emptive, response time, fairness, overhead, starvation risk)
    • How your algorithm compares to the other three
  • Prepare to use comparative language:
    • "Unlike FCFS…"
    • "Whereas Round Robin…"
    • "Both Priority and MLQ…"

🗣️ Part 2 — The Pitch & Challenge (18 minutes)

8 min
Poster Presentations: Students with posters stand in the four corners of the room (one corner per algorithm). Each poster designer gives a 2-minute explanation of their algorithm to the class.
8 min
The Challenge: The note-takers (Role B) come forward. They must compare their algorithm against another team's and explain why Microsoft should choose theirs.
Rule for the Challenge: You may not describe your algorithm in isolation. Every argument must reference at least one other algorithm — "unlike FCFS…", "whereas Round Robin…". This is the Compare command term in action.
2 min
The Decision: Sit down as a class. Vote on which algorithm won the contract. The winning team must have made the strongest comparative case — not just described their algorithm. 👉 Click here to celebrate!

Task 2 — Contract Bid 10 min

"Compare First-Come First-Served and Round Robin scheduling. [4 marks]"
📝 Before you write — access both Information Sheets:

You need to compare two algorithms, so you need to look at both Information Sheets. Open the FCFS sheet and the Round Robin sheet side by side. Use the "How X Compares to the Others" sections in each sheet to find your similarity, difference, consequence, and trade-off.

📄 Open FCFS Sheet 📄 Open Round Robin Sheet

📝 How to use the sentence frame:

The sentence frame below gives you the structure of a full-mark comparative answer. Fill in each blank with the correct detail:

  • First "Both ___ and ___" → name the two algorithms (FCFS and Round Robin)
  • "are ___" → state the similarity (both are CPU scheduling algorithms)
  • "However, ___ is ___ and runs ___" → name one algorithm, its pre-emptive status, and how it allocates the CPU
  • "whereas ___ is ___ and gives ___" → name the other algorithm and contrast it
  • "The effect of this difference is that ___" → the consequence for a running process (e.g. convoy effect vs even CPU sharing)
  • "___ pays for this with ___" → the trade-off (e.g. Round Robin pays with context-switch overhead)

Work through the frame in order. When you've filled every blank, you have a Level 4 comparative answer.

Plenary — Self-Assessment 5 min

Look at your Contract Bid answer. Decide which level you achieved — be honest.

Teacher reveals: Level 1–4 model answers

The question: "Compare First-Come First-Served and Round Robin scheduling. [4 marks]"

Level 1 — 1 mark (Descriptive, no comparison)

"FCFS runs processes in the order they arrive. It is non-pre-emptive. Round Robin gives each process a fixed time slice called a quantum. It is pre-emptive."

Why 1 mark: Describes each algorithm separately. No comparison. Doesn't refer to both throughout. This is the trap students fall into — describing instead of comparing.

Level 2 — 2 marks (Similarity AND difference, no consequence)

"Both FCFS and Round Robin are CPU scheduling algorithms. However, FCFS is non-pre-emptive and Round Robin is pre-emptive."

Why 2 marks: States a similarity (both are scheduling algorithms) and a difference (pre-emptive vs non-pre-emptive). But doesn't explain what the difference means or what the consequence is.

Level 3 — 3 marks (Similarity + difference + consequence)

"Both FCFS and Round Robin are CPU scheduling algorithms that decide the order in which processes run. However, FCFS is non-pre-emptive and runs each process to completion, whereas Round Robin is pre-emptive and gives each a fixed time slice before moving it to the back of the queue. The effect of this difference is that FCFS can make short processes wait behind a long one (the convoy effect), while Round Robin shares the CPU more evenly so short processes respond sooner."

Why 3 marks: Similarity + difference + consequence, all using comparative language. Missing the trade-off.

Level 4 — 4 marks (Similarity + difference + consequence + trade-off)

"Both FCFS and Round Robin are processor scheduling algorithms that decide the order in which ready processes use the CPU [1].

However, FCFS is non-pre-emptive and runs each process to completion, whereas Round Robin is pre-emptive and gives each process a fixed time slice before moving it to the back of the queue [1].

The effect of this difference is that FCFS can make short processes wait behind a long one (the convoy effect), while Round Robin shares the CPU more evenly so short processes respond sooner [1].

Round Robin pays for this with the overhead of frequent context switching, which FCFS largely avoids [1]."

Why 4 marks: Similarity + difference + consequence + trade-off, comparative throughout. This is the full-mark answer.

Mark allocation:

  • [1] — States a similarity (both are CPU scheduling algorithms)
  • [1] — States a difference (pre-emptive vs non-pre-emptive)
  • [1] — Links the difference to a consequence (convoy effect vs even CPU sharing)
  • [1] — Identifies the trade-off (Round Robin's context-switching overhead vs FCFS's avoidance of it)
Honest self-assessment: Decide which level your answer reached. Be ready to say why.

🙋 Show of hands: Who got Level 1? Level 2? Level 3? Level 4?

If you didn't use comparative language ("unlike", "whereas", "both… and…", "the effect of this difference is…"), you haven't hit the Compare standard — and that's the feedback you need for next time.
💡 Key Takeaway: "No scheduling approach is best in every way. FCFS is simple but non-pre-emptive and suffers the convoy effect; Round Robin is pre-emptive and responsive but pays in context-switching overhead; Priority serves urgent work first but can starve low-priority processes; Multilevel Queue separates workloads cleanly but can starve lower queues. To compare them is to say which is more responsive, which carries more overhead, which can starve — and to justify your choice for the workload in front of you."
